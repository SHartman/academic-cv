I grew up with a passion for dinosaurs and everything science. Growing up in Wisconsin and California, I had access to great museums but not many rock exposures, so for my undergraduate education I went to the University of Wyoming, where I am forever grateful for the field and museum experience I gained. Before graduate school I moved to Thermopolis, Wyoming, where I managed research, collections, and display development at the Wyoming Dinosaur Center. Around this time I also started producing what has become a large catalog of skeletal anatomy diagrams. Interested parties can explore them at [skeletaldrawing.com](https://www.skeletaldrawing.com).

My work at the WDC rekindled my drive to become a professional research scientist, so I relocated to Wisconsin to pursue a PhD in paleobiology. My dissertation research emphasized mechanistic physiological modeling, mass extinction survivorship, and functional anatomy. These days I am still in Wisconsin, where I teach those same subjects to undergraduates in the Department of Biology.

In addition to my academic pursuits, I have a wonderful wife and daughter and some fur-babies, and in what passes for free time I enjoy movies, photography, gaming, and cheering for the Green Bay Packers.
